---
title: Dialogs and layers
description: A cursor belongs to the layer its anchor sits in. A visibility policy decides what shows while a dialog is open.
---

A dialog covers the layout behind it, but a remote cursor can belong to either layer. Your teammate's cursor on a card behind your dialog, and your cursor inside a dialog the receiver has not opened, both need a rule. This demo runs that rule with a visibility policy. The cursor itself always paints above the dialog, because `CursorLayer` defaults to `z-index: 9999`.

<ExamplePreview scene="dialogs" />

## What's in the demo

- The wide pane runs a team board with an invite dialog. The narrow pane renders the board without the dialog.
- Mira opens the dialog with the context `{ dialog: "invite" }` and points at an anchor inside it. She closes it with the context `{ dialog: null }`. Theo stays on a card behind the dialog.
- While the wide dialog is open, the wide pane hides Theo's cursor: his anchor sits outside the dialog. The narrow pane hides Mira's cursor: her context names a dialog this pane has not opened.
- Your cursor follows the same rule: while the dialog is open, it only shows where a dialog exists. The open pane hides your cursor on the board behind the dialog. The other pane hides your cursor, because your context names a dialog it has not opened.
- A cursor inside the dialog paints above the dialog, because the cursor's z-index beats the dialog's.
- The Invite button opens the dialog and its close button closes it. It stays open while Mira or you have it open.
